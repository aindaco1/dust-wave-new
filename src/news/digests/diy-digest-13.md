Welcome to this week's *DIY Filmmaker Digest* — part syllabus, part mixtape, part fever dream of cinema's shifting ground. Below: essays and interviews that ask what artists are tinkering with, what systems are breaking, and how you might improvise your own way forward. There's a **major** New Mexico bias, plus podcasts, videos, and trailers to keep your queue weird. **DIY forever!**

But first: we're going to brag for a second. **Dust Wave built its own crowdfunding platform, and in 2026 it went 4-for-4.**

> **Four campaigns. Four goals met. $10,601 raised against $9,500 in combined goals.**

We say "don't wait for permission" a lot. Sometimes that means making the film. Other times it means building the annoying infrastructure around it because the existing options aren't doing what you need. That's how <a href="https://pool.dustwave.xyz/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>The Pool</strong></a> happened. It's Dust Wave's free, open-source, all-or-nothing crowdfunding system: supporters pledge without making accounts, cards are charged only when a campaign reaches its goal, and creators keep their campaign site under their own control. The code, documentation, and the whole slightly unruly machine are available at <a href="https://thepool.fund/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>thepool.fund</strong></a>.

This year, four projects used it to get funded. So: not a theory anymore.

That's what we're proud of -- <strong>The Pool isn't a pitch deck or a promise about what we might build someday. It works.</strong> We built it, ran it, and watched four projects cross the line with it this year. And because it's free and open source, somebody else can take it apart, rebuild it, and make it serve their own community. DIY forever sometimes means owning the means of production. Sometimes, less glamorously, it means owning the checkout flow.

More campaigns to come!
